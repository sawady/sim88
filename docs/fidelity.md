# Fidelity to MSX88

SIM88 follows MSX88 V-3.1 (R. de Diego, UPM), the simulator many computer
architecture courses use, and its assembler ASM88. Where MSX88 does something,
SIM88 does the same, even when a real 8086 would not (decision 5 in
[PROGRESS.md](../PROGRESS.md)).

This is checked against **the original program**: the programs in
[`tests/fidelity/programs`](../tests/fidelity/programs) run on MSX88, ASM88 and
LINK88 in an emulated PC ([`tools/oracle`](../tools/oracle/README.md)), and
what they did (the `.LST` listing, the registers, the flags, the memory and the
screen) is kept in [`tests/fidelity/golden`](../tests/fidelity/golden). The
test suite runs the same programs on SIM88 and compares
([`fidelity.test.js`](../src/model/__tests__/fidelity.test.js)); a program that
differs must be listed, with the reason, in
[`known-differences.json`](../tests/fidelity/known-differences.json).

Programs whose name starts with `exp-` are experiments about timing: they run
on MSX88 but are not compared (their results depend on the wall clock).

The corpus runs with the ALU **as MSX88**, the default setting. With
**Options › ALU: as 8086** eight programs differ, all because of the four cases
below: `alu-adc-sbb-flags`, `alu-add-adc-8`, `alu-inc-dec-flags`,
`alu-inc-dec-neg`, `alu-logic`, `alu-not-flags`, `stack` and `stack-push-sp`.

## Where MSX88 and the 8086 differ: the ALU setting

The original MSX88 departs from a real 8086 (and, for `NOT`, from its own
manual) in four places. SIM88 copies MSX88 by default, because then results
match the original simulator that course exercises and listings are checked
against. The setting **Options › ALU** (`aluMode` in the settings, `'msx88'` or
`'8086'`) switches to the 8086 behaviour, to discuss the difference in class;
**Help › Differences from the 8086** shows the same table in the interface.

| Instruction | As MSX88 (default) | As a real 8086 | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INC`, `DEC` | clear C | leave C alone | `alu-inc-dec-flags`, `alu-inc-dec-neg` |
| `ADC`, `SBB` with C = 1 | `ADD` / `SUB`, then `INC` / `DEC` of the result, with the flags of that last step: C ends 0 (and A can differ) | one addition / subtraction with the carry: C, O and A of the whole result | `alu-adc-sbb-flags`, `alu-add-adc-8` |
| `NOT` | 8 bits: Z = P = 1, C O S A = 0; 16 bits: all six 0 | changes no flag (also what the MSX88 manual says) | `alu-not-flags`, `alu-logic` |
| `PUSH SP` | pushes 0000h | pushes SP after the decrement (SP − 2); the 80286 and later push it before | `stack-push-sp`, `stack` |

In code: `src/model/alu.ts` exports both sets of operations (`ALU_MODES`), and
`execute()` / `step()` in `src/model/cpu.ts` take `{ aluMode }`; the machine
keeps the mode as a setting (stepping back does not undo it).

## By area

"Identical" means every program of the area gives the same bytes, registers,
flags, memory and screen as MSX88.

## The printer's paper

Programs with `;; paper: yes` compare the lines of the paper (F9, F5). What
they show:

- The HAND's interrupt line is level triggered, and the STROBE of the PIO
  takes the character on its rising edge: `printer-hand-int`, `printer-pio`,
  `printer-pio-edge`.
- The paper has 20 columns (`printer-wrap`) and shows the last seven lines
  (`printer-lines`).
- **Pending, two differences:** MSX88 ignores CR and only LF starts a new line,
  while SIM88 treats CR, LF and CR LF as one new line (`printer-crlf`). And the
  CDMA in block mode to the printer only gets the first four characters on the
  paper, the size of its buffer, while SIM88 waits for DREQ and prints them all
  (`printer-cdma-block`); on demand both print everything
  (`printer-cdma-demand`). The model stays as it is until these are decided:
  see open questions 20 and 21 in PROGRESS.md.

## Time: the timer and the printer

The timer's clock on MSX88 is **real time**: the same program, in fast mode,
counts 415 and 444 loop turns before the first tick in two runs
(`exp-timer-speed-fast`, `exp-timer-speed-fast-again`; 435 and 470 in a second
run of the whole corpus), and about three times as many with COMP = 3
(`exp-timer-speed-comp3`: 1270, then 1417). With an instruction-based
clock both runs would give the same count. In the slow (animated) mode a
program does not even reach `STI` in 20 seconds.

The printer also prints in real time: after four characters sent as fast as
the program can, BUSY is 1 (`exp-printer-buffer`), while one character alone
never sets it (`exp-printer-busy`).

SIM88 keeps the timer and the printer on settings of their own (frequency and
printer speed, in the interface) rather than on the wall clock; that is a
design choice of the interface, not of the model.

## Not covered yet

- Step by step traces (F6/F7): only the state at the end is compared.
- 8086 short forms (`A0`-`A3`, `40+r`, accumulator immediates) as raw bytes:
  ASM88 never emits them and it is not known whether MSX88 runs them.
- `BP`, `SI` and `DI` as register operands in raw bytes.
